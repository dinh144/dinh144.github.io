## Answers

### Best proven directions
1. Explorable stylised world / overworld map (Messenger 7.92, Overworld Audio 7.38, PeterOravec HM, Zesiger HM). Highest scores, most direct fit.
2. Quest / RPG vocabulary on real content (Grail 7.35 SOTD+Developer; Dont Look Up 7.25). Scores come from content and creativity, not heavy UI.
3. Cosy pixel / retro-OS framing (Cozy Eating Village HM, YOYU, Block Rage, PS2/Olivia OS nominees). Many entries, few top scores; works as an accent.
Note: usability is the lowest sub-score on every SOTD game site (6.96 to 7.46). That is the readability risk, measured.

### Risky for a reading blog
- Full playable/driving worlds as the only way in (Awards Racing, Zesiger): gating text behind controls, keyboard and motion issues.
- Neon arcade / cyberpunk-Tokyo night (Night Drive, Game Gallery): low contrast, glare, and cliche; fails the 4.5:1 floor easily.
- Heavy pixel fonts for body text; WebGPU/real-time 3D on phones (battery, reduced motion).
- No award-level evidence at all for ukiyo-e or Japanese editorial typography in this index, so treat those as unproven here.

### Vietnamese personal touch (INFERRED suggestions)
- World direction: regions as Vietnamese places (hẻm, chợ nổi, ruộng bậc thang, phố cổ lanterns) drawn in a soft pixel or flat style, ap-southeast-1 as the "home hẻm"; Sài Gòn plastic-stool and phin-coffee details as props (PHIN PHIN proves the register).
- Quest direction: series = quest lines, posts = "nhiệm vụ", reading progress as XP; all labels with proper diacritics (test ệ ữ ặ ở).
- Cosy pixel direction: pastel lamp-lit village with lantern (đèn lồng) and áo dài-red/lotus-pink accents instead of sakura; keep article panel flat, paper-toned, system-grade serif/sans.
- Cross-cutting: always offer the Daiki Fujita pattern (plain reading mode alongside the world), consistent with our motion contract.

Motion findings (get_motion_dna = SEEN numbers; filmstrip = SEEN images; the rest INFERRED):
- Messenger: filmstrip shows a preloader: a hand-drawn envelope icon with "LOADING", fading in and out, then a teal shape wipes in at the bottom. DNA: 8 CSS keyframes, no GSAP/ScrollTrigger, no scroll model detected, 1000 ms median duration. The world itself is canvas-driven (INFERRED), so DOM motion tooling sees little.
- Overworld Audio: filmstrip shows only a dark "OVERWORLD AUDIO" wordmark with a thin progress line in the first frame (preloader), then static; the three.js scene was not visible in the 12 frames. DNA: three + webgl, 6 CSS keyframes, no ScrollTrigger, 300 to 500 ms durations (short UI transitions).
- Grail: Lenis smooth scroll + WebGL, 90 CSS keyframes, one easing cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1), durations 500 to 620 ms. Filmstrip shows chapter-based scroll ("Chapter 01..08" labels), full-bleed colour changes per chapter (cream, black, lilac, brown, off-white), headline serif lines reveal, floating 3D cards drifting, and an early-access email field at the end. Quest copy ("Quest accepted. Clock's running.") carries the game idea; the UI itself is quiet.
- Dont Look Up: three + webgl, 66 CSS keyframes, 5000 ms median (looping ambient animation). No scroll model; it is a game screen, not a document.
- PeterOravec: webgl, no DOM animation census at all (canvas-only). Filmstrip shows a pixel-character preloader with a percentage bar (59% to 100%) on a white page, then the frames stay blank/black in the recording window, so the world itself was not captured. Mobile capture exists; check the PNG by hand.

Take-aways for our blog (INFERRED):
- Preloaders on game sites are tiny, themed and short (envelope, pixel avatar + percentage). For ours: show real content first, per the motion contract; a themed loader only as an optional enhancement.
- The two scored scroll sites (Grail, Overworld) use short eases (300 to 620 ms) with one signature curve; a single easing token is enough.
- Canvas-only worlds (PeterOravec, Messenger) expose almost nothing to DOM tooling, which hints at the accessibility risk: our world needs a real DOM link layer under the canvas.
- Two captures timed out on heavy WebGL pages (Messenger mobile, Dont Look Up both), consistent with the weight risk on phones.
