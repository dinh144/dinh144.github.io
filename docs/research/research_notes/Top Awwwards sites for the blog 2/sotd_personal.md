# Personal-site Awwwards SOTD winners 2025-2026 (excluding already-studied sites and SOTY/SOTM)

Method and limits (read first)
- Firecrawl ran out of credits mid-task (resets 2026-11-10); everything else was fetched through Exa. The sandbox blocks direct curl to awwwards.com. I could NOT open the live sites, so stack/markup facts come from Awwwards tags and from third-party teardowns (rickcreates.com, which measures assets in a real browser), not from my own view-source. Facts are marked SEEN (on a page I fetched) or INFERRED.
- Awwwards scoring: Design 40 / Usability 30 / Creativity 20 / Content 10; Developer Award sub-scores are Semantics/SEO, Animations, Accessibility, WPO, Responsive, Markup. Source: [Awwwards G. Colombel page showing the weights](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/g-colombel-portfolio-2026).
- Scale reality: personal-site SOTDs in 2025-2026 mostly sit at 7.2-7.8. Only one personal project I found passed 8.0 (Sleep Well Creative, 8.03, a personal essay site, not a portfolio). A "7.5+" bar leaves only about 5 personal sites in range, so the list below also includes 7.2-7.5 sites that matter for a blog (Cyd Stumpel, Hiroto Sato, Dylan Brouwer).
- Date caveat: some pages I fetched (Justine Soulie 7.58, Axel Vanhessche 7.80, Jenny Johannesson 7.73, Gilles Tossoukpe 7.57, Richard Ekwonye 7.44, Eduard Bodak 7.39) did not show an SOTD date. I did not verify they are 2025-2026, so they are in the Gaps list, not the main table.

## Q2. Which have a writing/blog/notes/journal section, and how is reading designed?

### Takeaway
Only Cyd Stumpel's site has a full blog; Jesper Landberg has a newsletter of "occasional front-end notes"; Sleep Well Creative is itself long-form reading. Almost none of the high scorers are built around text, so reading-experience references must be borrowed from the two text-bearing ones.

### Cited Findings
- Cyd Stumpel: blog index lists dated posts (e.g. 2026-07-08, 2026-07-03, 2025-12-13) with webmention counts; reading time shown per post ("Reading time: 3 minutes"), byline and date at top, code samples with CodePen embeds — [Blogs page](https://cydstumpel.nl/blogs/), [example post](https://cydstumpel.nl/two-approaches-to-fallback-css-scroll-driven-animations/). SEEN.
- Cyd's site is "built with the newest CSS techniques: View Transitions and Scroll Driven Animations" and "Powered by CSS" with a blog describing the implementation — [OKAY DEV](https://okaydev.co/u/cyd/portfolio/portfolio-2025).
- Jesper Landberg: NEWSLETTER corner opens the same black-hole overlay with a short note and email field for "occasional front-end notes"; FULL view lists all 23 projects as one centred text passage like a table of contents — [rickcreates teardown](https://rickcreates.com/r/2026-09-29-jesper-landberg).
- Sleep Well Creative: six numbered chapters on navy/cream pages alternating with full-screen 3D scenes; titles in PP Editorial New light serif, body in Neue Montreal, body about 24 px and titles about 87 px on 1920 px; background colour flips at each topic change; ten percent paper-grain overlay — [rickcreates](https://rickcreates.com/r/2026-02-07-sleep-well-creative). Titles fade in letter by letter (each letter about 2 s, 20 ms stagger) so a 100-letter title takes over 4 s; the teardown notes chapter titles are not heading elements.
- Jesper Landberg publishes an `llms.txt` for AI tools describing how he works — [rickcreates](https://rickcreates.com/r/2026-09-29-jesper-landberg).

### Inferences
- Reading pattern worth copying: alternate two background "inks" per topic (Sleep Well), keep reading chrome minimal (Cyd's reading-time + date + webmentions). Slow letter-by-letter reveals are a bad fit for blog body text because 4 s title reveals hurt reading speed (INFERRED).

### Gaps
- I did not open live article pages other than a Cyd Stumpel post, so typography/measure of her article pages is not measured.
- No evidence found of a Vietnamese/bilingual SOTD personal site; Hiroto Sato has an EN/JA toggle (a bilingual precedent) but I did not inspect its implementation.

## Q3. What separates 7.8+ from 7.2-7.5 personal sites?

### Takeaway
No personal site in range reaches 7.8 SOTD; the visible gap between 7.7 sites and 7.2-7.3 sites is Creativity (about 8.0 vs 7.1-7.5) and Usability is the weakest category everywhere (7.0-7.5).

### Cited Findings
- Creativity is the top category on the best sites: Sleep Well 8.35, Pacome 8.09, Stas Bondar 8.08, Jesper 8.04, Huy Phan 7.78 (Design 7.91) — see table sources above. Lower-scoring sites: Dylan Brouwer 7.14, Artiom 7.33, Brandon Yasin 7.44, Elliott Mangham 7.59.
- Usability range across all fetched personal sites: 6.92 (Elliott Mangham) to 7.80 (Sleep Well); nearly every site's lowest category is Usability — Awwwards pages cited above.
- Jury splits are wide: on G. Colombel, individual jurors gave 6.1 to 9.6 on the same site; the Awwwards Users (Pro/Chief) average typically gives 8-10 while jurors give 7s — e.g. Jesper Landberg users 9.47 vs jury categories 7.5-8.0 — [Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/jesper-landberg-4). Rule: the system discards the three scores furthest from the average and requires at least 18 jury members — [Liuhuo Stack](https://stack.liuhuo.org/en/discover/awwwards).
- Concept pattern of 7.6+ sites: one memorable metaphor that is the interface — Jesper's bending ribbon + black hole, Sleep Well's picture book, Pacome's spiral gallery — and very short content ("two-sentence introduction") — [rickcreates](https://rickcreates.com/r/2026-09-29-jesper-landberg). The 7.2-7.3 group includes conventional gallery + about layouts (Artiom Yakushev, Olha Lazarieva with GSAP/React/Webflow).
- rickcreates on Jesper's low Usability: every entry point is small text in a corner and keyboard focus is invisible in the first half of the Tab order.

### Inferences
- A blog that wins on Creativity needs a single distinctive device (not many effects) while keeping Usability at 7.5+, which for a blog means obvious navigation and readable text. INFERRED from the pattern above, not from jury comments (Awwwards does not publish comments).

### Gaps
- Awwwards does not publish jury comments, so reasons behind scores are inference.

## Q4. Accessibility, performance, reduced-motion handling

### Takeaway
Accessibility is the lowest Dev sub-score on nearly every site (6.2-7.8); WPO is typically 7.2-8.6. Reduced-motion handling is rarely evidenced on the Awwwards side; the best-documented good practice is Cyd Stumpel's.

### Cited Findings
- Accessibility sub-scores: Jesper 7.0, Sleep Well 6.4, Nick Ho 6.2, Gianluca 6.8, Elliott 7.0, Olha 7.0, Brandon Yasin 7.2, Cyd Stumpel 7.8 (highest), G. Colombel 7.6, Stefan Vitasovic 7.0 — Awwwards pages cited in Q1.
- WPO sub-scores: Jesper 8.6, Stefan Vitasovic 8.4, Brandon Yasin 8.2, Elliott 8.2, Gianluca 7.8, Leo Parpeix 7.2 — same sources.
- Measured page weight (rickcreates, real browser): Jesper Landberg 34.6 MB within about 13 s, 26.5 MB of it six project films, about 37 MB on all devices, FCP 0.10-0.11 s, CLS 0.0006, 60 fps; Leo Parpeix about 31 MB desktop / 17 MB phones, FCP 0.23-0.59 s, CLS 0; Sleep Well about 44.6 MB (40.9 MB video), 25.5 MB before "Enter Site", FCP 676-788 ms, CLS 0.014 — [Jesper](https://rickcreates.com/r/2026-09-29-jesper-landberg), [Parpeix](https://rickcreates.com/r/2026-09-28-leo-parpeix-portfolio), [Sleep Well](https://rickcreates.com/r/2026-02-07-sleep-well-creative). Takeaway: Dev Award is awarded despite 30-45 MB pages, so WPO jurors do not penalise heavy WebGL if first paint is fast.
- Sleep Well Creative: declares no `lang`, chapter titles are not heading elements (screen reader finds only three top-level headings), does not read `prefers-reduced-motion`; Accessibility score 6.4, still won Dev 7.42 — [rickcreates](https://rickcreates.com/r/2026-02-07-sleep-well-creative).
- Leo Parpeix and Jesper Landberg: rickcreates notes a single landmark, no visible keyboard focus (Parpeix) and invisible focus early in tab order (Jesper); both scored Accessibility 7.0 — [Parpeix](https://rickcreates.com/r/2026-09-28-leo-parpeix-portfolio), [Jesper](https://rickcreates.com/r/2026-09-29-jesper-landberg).
- Cyd Stumpel's approach: reduced-motion query is the first thing in her scroll-driven CSS ("Don't add animation if people have reduced motion preference... encase your code in one single thing"); progressive enhancement via `@supports (animation-timeline: view())`, with GSAP ScrollTrigger fallback chosen via `CSS.supports("animation-timeline","view()")` for the portfolio itself — [talk transcript](https://beyondtellerrand.com/events/dusseldorf-2025/speakers/cyd-stumpel), [her post](https://cydstumpel.nl/two-approaches-to-fallback-css-scroll-driven-animations/).
- Third-party review guidance: toggle `prefers-reduced-motion` and check the fallback; award sites are tested on throttled real devices — [Hon Tran](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/best-award-winning-websites-2026) (opinion piece, single author).
- Julian Neagu's 3D portfolio (not an SOTD, example only): three version switcher (classic / immersive / cinematic), phones get smaller textures (10.8 MB to 4.9 MB), reduced motion shows static scenes — [dev.to](https://dev.to/julianneagu/i-rebuilt-my-portfolio-as-a-3d-world-you-fly-through-heres-what-i-learned-2nl).
- Earlier precedent (2023, outside range, useful pattern): Yannis Yannakopoulos ships a WebGL mode, an "experimental" mode and an accessibility mode; the light version loads on `prefers-reduced-motion` (reacts live when toggled) and on mobile — [Awwwards case](https://www.awwwards.com/portfolio-of-yannis-yannakopoulos-v2.html).
- Mobile behaviour: Jesper turns the horizontal ribbon into a vertical card column on phones and moves the close button to the bottom right; tablet keeps the desktop ribbon and shrinks corner links to about 40 percent size (hard to read) — [rickcreates](https://rickcreates.com/r/2026-09-29-jesper-landberg). Sleep Well keeps all 3D scenes on phones, reframed, single column, custom cursor removed on touch.

### Inferences
- Because Accessibility is the lowest sub-score on almost all winners, a blog that has real landmarks, headings, visible focus and a reduced-motion path would already beat the cohort on that axis (Cyd Stumpel 7.8 is the evidence that it moves the score).
- Heavy assets are tolerated on Dev scoring if the page paints fast, but a text blog has no need to approach 30 MB; target under 1 MB for reading pages (INFERRED recommendation).

### Gaps
- I did not run Lighthouse or inspect live markup/`prefers-reduced-motion` on any site; only Sleep Well, Parpeix, Jesper and Cyd have third-party statements.
- No data found on Pacome Pertant, Huy Phan, Stas Bondar, Brandon Yasin for reduced motion.

## Things a blog could borrow (concrete)
- Jesper Landberg: black ground, white type, colour only from content; tiny all-caps corner navigation (but make targets 44 px and focus visible); newsletter as an overlay with a rainbow-border focus ring on the email field; project index as a plain text list "FULL" view — [rickcreates](https://rickcreates.com/r/2026-09-29-jesper-landberg).
- Sleep Well Creative: two-ink chapters (navy/cream) with background flip per topic, serif for slow lines + grotesque for body, sizes scaling proportionally to viewport width, ten percent paper grain, a "THE NOTE" letter modal — [rickcreates](https://rickcreates.com/r/2026-02-07-sleep-well-creative). Avoid: no `lang`, no heading elements, no reduced-motion.
- Cyd Stumpel: CSS-first motion (View Transitions + scroll-driven animations) with `@supports` fallback, reduced-motion wrapper first, blog list with date, reading time and webmention counts — fits a Pages-hosted Astro blog.
- Hiroto Sato: bilingual EN/JA toggle on About; a single 3D idea with restraint, quiet About page — [Marconato](https://riccardomarconato.com/blog/hiroto-sato-3d-street-signs-portfolio).
- Brandon Yasin: palette that shifts with time of day plus draggable clock; Awwwards element "Editorial Menu" like magazine covers side by side — [Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/brandon-yasin).
- Yannis Yannakopoulos pattern (2023): three modes, with the accessible/light one used for reduced motion and mobile, switching live.
